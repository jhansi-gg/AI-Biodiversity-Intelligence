CLIMATE FACTORS AND BIODIVERSITY

Temperature and rainfall are important environmental factors that influence ecosystems, plant growth, water availability, and species survival.

Temperature affects plant development, animal activity, reproduction, and the distribution of many species. Changes in temperature can alter suitable habitat conditions.

Rainfall influences soil moisture, surface water availability, vegetation growth, and ecosystem productivity. Low rainfall can increase water stress, especially in semi-arid environments.

Climate conditions interact with soil properties and land use. For example, low rainfall combined with low soil organic carbon may increase concern about water availability and soil functions.

Extreme heat and irregular rainfall can create additional stress for plants and wildlife. The effects depend on ecosystem type, species characteristics, and local environmental conditions.

Environmental reasoning:
Temperature, rainfall, and soil conditions should be considered together when assessing environmental risks. Management practices that improve vegetation cover, soil water retention, and habitat availability may help reduce some climate-related environmental stresses.

Important metrics:
- Temperature
- Rainfall
- Soil moisture
- Water availability
- Vegetation productivity
- Species survival

Source category:
Climate science literature, environmental assessments, and climate reports.

Note:
Climate impacts vary by location, ecosystem, species, and time period. Recommendations should use local conditions and relevant scientific evidence rather than assuming the same impact everywhere.
