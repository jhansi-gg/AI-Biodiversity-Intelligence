HUMAN IMPACT ON BIODIVERSITY

Human activities can influence soil health, water quality, habitat availability, and biodiversity.

Deforestation removes vegetation and can reduce habitat availability for wildlife. It can also affect soil conditions, water cycles, and ecosystem functions.

Pollution can affect soil, water, plants, animals, and microorganisms. The type and severity of impacts depend on the pollutant, concentration, exposure, and environmental conditions.

Intensive land use can place pressure on natural habitats and may contribute to habitat fragmentation and biodiversity loss.

Agricultural practices can influence soil organic carbon, soil biological activity, water availability, and habitat conditions.

Environmental reasoning:
Human impacts should be considered together with climate, soil, and land-use conditions. For example, habitat fragmentation combined with low vegetation diversity and water stress may create multiple pressures on biodiversity.

Important metrics:
- Deforestation
- Pollution level
- Habitat loss
- Habitat fragmentation
- Soil degradation
- Water quality
- Biodiversity indicators

Source category:
Environmental research, biodiversity assessments, pollution studies, and environmental reports.

Note:
Environmental impacts vary according to location, pollutant type, land-use practices, ecosystem characteristics, and duration. Recommendations should be based on relevant scientific evidence and local conditions.
